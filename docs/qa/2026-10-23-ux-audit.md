# Gameplay UX audit · 2026-10-23

## Scope and evidence

Read-only product audit; no gameplay or styling changes. Stack: DOM/CSS client, authoritative Node/ws server. Used router → game-ui-ux + game-playtest. Goal: identify gaps in text, buttons, highlights, animation and contextual help. Approach: review the live player flow and compare runtime rendering with rules/state and the design contract. Risks: ten-seat density and unusual powers need dedicated state coverage. Verification: local browser create → bot → ready → start → bid → inspect → play → trick → round → ledger → next round at 320×568 and 390×844; source inspection of remaining phases, powers, settings and victory. Existing server occupied port 3001 and served the expected game. No deployment, physical touch, screen-reader, desktop or ten-seat visual certification in this pass. A browser confirm interrupted further browser QA; do not interpret untested states as passing.

## Already present

- Clear primary/secondary button styling, focus-visible outlines and native dialog semantics.
- Suit colors/symbols, underlined 6/9, pirate/Mermaid framing, declared Tigress role and public-card enlargement.
- Mint active-turn cue separate from gold current-leader styling; optional turn chime and vibration.
- Inspect-before-play alternative to swiping, legality validation, forced-card marking.
- Sealed bidding, explicit lock, waiting names and counts.
- General rules, card library, score/history tables, bonus breakdowns.
- Result panels, podium and final victory treatment; reduced-motion styles for major effects.

These are useful foundations, not evidence that every intended feedback feature is wired or sufficient.

## P2 · address first

1. **Study cards look unavailable during bidding.** Observed at both phone sizes: every hand card is dimmed and stamped WAIT TURN while the instruction asks the player to study it. `app.js:80` treats every non-play phase as a blocked turn. Use a neutral inspectable hand during bidding; reserve restrictive overlays for actual play restrictions.

2. **Keyboard focus is lost on authoritative updates.** Observed focus returning to the document after locking a bid, playing and advancing the round. `render()` replaces hand/table controls and `renderPhase()` replaces phase controls without restoring focus; `inspect()` leaves focus on the hand card even though its actions are earlier in DOM order. Preserve focus by stable action/card identity, focus the new phase heading when necessary, and return focus on inspection close. Phase/turn headings also lack a dedicated live announcement.

3. **Power decisions are immediate commitments with little context.** Source-confirmed: `app.js:92–95` supplies a title and options; a click immediately sends the choice. There is no power description, source illustration, consequence preview or selection/confirm step. This is especially weak for discarding, wagers and forced cards. Keep simple Continue immediate; use explicit selection/confirmation for consequential choices and concise timing/cost text.

4. **Same-kind power chains have no distinct transition identity.** `lastEvent` uses round/trick/phase/turn/play-count but no decision identity/revision. Consecutive decisions can retain the same signature, particularly First Mate chains and Bendt discards. Combined with wholesale panel replacement, there is no reliable new-step focus or outcome announcement. Give each decision a stable step identity and identify which captured power is resolving.

5. **Turn guidance omits the information needed to decide.** `app.js:75` displays only cards played and cards dealt. Show established lead suit, current winning card/player, local won/bid deficit or overshoot, and remaining participants. Label the gold leader state explicitly; distinguish lead suit from current winner. Avoid labeling another player's empty slot “Your turn” (`app.js:78`).

6. **Restriction labels can give the wrong reason.** `app.js:80` stamps every locally blocked card FOLLOW SUIT when it is the player's turn. A Mary-forced-card restriction is not a follow-suit restriction. Derive the visible reason from legality and give forced cards a short “Mary chose this” label. Keep the exact explanation available on inspection.

7. **Results obscure the battle they explain.** Observed at 320px: the large trick panel covers much of the table, including ownership/stats, while the winner heading repeats the banner. Prefer a compact result tray with the winning play visible and an expandable explanation; show captures/removals where they occurred.

8. **Round scoring hides the useful explanation.** Observed: round completion shows cumulative totals, with bid points and bonus explanation behind View scores & bonuses. Show a personal summary directly: won/bid, bid points, bonus, round delta and new total. Mark exact/missed bids with text/icons rather than only podium rank.

9. **Mobile ledger hides columns without advertising horizontal scroll.** Observed at 320px: Round and Total extend beyond the visible table. Keep the player column anchored and provide a scroll cue or a mobile row-detail layout. Expand bonus reasons from the current result, rather than requiring a second history disclosure.

10. **Live card/deal animations are not wired.** Source-confirmed: CSS defines `.arriving`, `.dealing` and card landing effects, but `app.js` never applies those classes. Cards are recreated on snapshots instead. Animate only new plays/deals keyed by stable card IDs, avoiding replay on reconnect or unrelated room updates.

11. **Rule-changing events lack event-specific visual feedback.** Runtime uses a generic result seal and sound. Plank removed IDs and result bonuses exist, but the renderer leaves removed cards visually intact and does not show capture/Locker/creature outcomes at their cards. Add short labeled removal/destruction/capture cues with a static reduced-motion equivalent. Mary selection, bid changes, wagers and next-leader choices also need persistent outcome text; do not expose private cards to other seats.

12. **Action errors can strand disabled controls.** Source-confirmed: bid/choice/continue buttons disable on send; `data.type==='error'` only shows a notice and usually does not rerender. A stale revision or rejected action can leave the control unavailable until another state arrives. Restore controls from the authoritative state on rejection, show the error beside the relevant action and retain the user's valid selection where possible.

## P3 · polish and teaching

13. **Bidding uses a large empty panel while the hand is small.** Observed at 390×844: most space sits between Lock bid and the crew status; cards remain small and dim below. Compact the chooser, make studying cards more prominent, and show the selected bid's scoring stakes (including zero).

14. **Card descriptions are dense paragraphs.** Observed Black 14 inspection; pirate descriptions are longer in `cards.js`. Split into Role / When / Effect / Bonus. Promote the immediate legal action and explain the bonus asterisk inline. Choice buttons should read “Play as Pirate,” “Play as Escape,” “Play as 14,” rather than raw values.

15. **Small text and touch targets remain.** At 320px, hand labels, seat progress and helper text are tiny; CSS includes 34–36px bidding/header controls and sub-10px functional text. Use 44px hit areas where feasible, improve the critical text hierarchy, and provide an accessible player/detail view for dense tables. Ten-seat late-round readability needs fresh screenshots, not only bounds checks.

16. **Lobby teaching is incomplete.** Source-confirmed: no How to play control in the lobby or settings; mode buttons lack descriptions; disabled start uses the generic “Start when everyone is ready.” Add a lobby guide, explain what Full expansion enables, and identify missing seats/readiness. Surface the two-player exclusions and Mary house rule where players choose the mode.

17. **Join form silently ignores incomplete codes.** `app.js:44` leaves Join enabled and returns without feedback for fewer than five letters. Disable until valid or show an inline five-letter requirement; support Enter submission. Show a pending state for create/join to discourage duplicate requests.

18. **Card library is not scoped to the game.** `cardGuide()` always shows every special, with no Base/Expansion/All filter or current-mode cue. Add filtering, retain scroll position on Back, and provide a route back to How to play. The active game should make unavailable cards clearly distinguishable.

19. **Feedback controls and recovery copy need precision.** Settings offer no turn-alert test despite the design contract. Host transfer and bot takeover appear only through changed state, without an explicit transition notice. Reconnect copy promises “Your seat is saved” without explaining the finite grace period or server restart limitations. Add an alert preview, visible takeover/host messages and accurate recovery status.

20. **Ranking decoration implies order among tied scores.** `podium()` and seat crowns use sorted position, while the final winner logic correctly includes all tied winners. Use shared ranks/crowns for ties so a silver podium does not imply a lower score.

## Recommended implementation order

1. Focus/action recovery, accurate legality labels and neutral bidding inspection.
2. Decision context/confirmation and stable power-step feedback.
3. Compact result tray, personal score explanation and mobile ledger.
4. Lead/bid guidance and event-keyed gameplay animation.
5. Lobby/library teaching, typography/touch targets and lifecycle notices.

Before calling the polish complete: verify 2/4/6/10 seats at 320px/390px/desktop, ten-card hands, long names, every power and chained First Mate decisions, rejected/stale actions, reconnect/bot takeover, ties, reduced motion, keyboard-only navigation and physical-phone swipe/audio/haptics. This audit identifies concrete gaps; it cannot prove that no other weakness exists.
